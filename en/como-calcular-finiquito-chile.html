How to calculate a termination (finiquito) in Chile 2026 (step by step)

Updated: July 2026 · 6 min read
In short: calculating a termination in Chile has four possible components: proportional vacation, indemnity for years of service (only in dismissal for company needs), indemnity in lieu of notice (if 30 days' notice was not given) and the proportional legal bonus. The grounds for termination define which apply. Below is the step-by-step with an example.

The finiquito is the document that ends the employment relationship and details all pending payments to the worker. How much you're owed depends, above all, on why the contract ends. Let's break it down.

Step 1: Identify the grounds for termination

The grounds determine whether there is indemnity or not:

  • Company needs (art. 161): entitles you to indemnity for years of service and, if no 30 days' notice was given, to the indemnity in lieu of notice.
  • Voluntary resignation or mutual agreement (art. 159): no indemnity for years of service or notice. Only proportional vacation, bonus and pending earnings.
  • Cause attributable to the worker (art. 160): also carries no indemnity for years of service.

Step 2: Define the calculation base

Indemnities are calculated on the last monthly salary, which includes base salary, benefits in kind and the average of variable pay (commissions or bonuses from recent months). Meal and transport allowances and one-off benefits are not included.

Base = base salary + legal bonus + average variable  ·  capped at 90 UF

Important: for indemnities this base has a legal cap of 90 UF. Vacation, by contrast, is paid on the full salary without that cap.

Step 3: Calculate proportional vacation

Legal leave is 15 business days per year, equivalent to 1.25 business days per month worked. Proportional vacation corresponds to the time worked since the last contract anniversary that you haven't taken yet.

Business days = 1.25 × months worked (since the last anniversary)

Those business days are then valued according to your daily wage (including the non-working days that fall within the vacation period).

Step 4: Calculate the indemnity for years of service

It only applies in a dismissal for company needs and if the contract was in force for one year or more. It equals:

30 days of the last salary × each year (and fraction > 6 months)  ·  cap 11 years

In practice, it's as many "monthly salaries" as years worked, up to a maximum of 11.

Step 5: Add the notice period (if applicable)

If the dismissal is for company needs and you were not given at least 30 days' notice, one additional monthly salary is added as indemnity in lieu of notice.

Step 6: Add the proportional legal bonus

If your employer doesn't pay the legal bonus (gratificación) monthly, a proportional part of the current year may be pending. The legal bonus has an annual cap of 4.75 monthly minimum wages, prorated across the months worked in the year.

Frequently asked questions

How is the indemnity for years of service calculated?

30 days of the last salary per year of service and any fraction over six months, capped at 11 years and a maximum base of 90 UF. Only in dismissal for company needs (art. 161) with one year or more of contract.

How is proportional vacation calculated?

15 business days per year = 1.25 business days per month worked since the last anniversary, valued according to the daily wage.

What is the termination calculation base?

The last monthly salary: base salary + benefits in kind + average of variable pay. For indemnities it's capped at 90 UF.

Is there indemnity in a resignation?

No. In a resignation (art. 159) only proportional vacation, the proportional bonus and pending wages apply.
